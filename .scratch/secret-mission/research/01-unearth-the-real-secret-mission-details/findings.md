# Findings: Unearthing the real "Secret Mission" (Alcatel One Touch 311, Romania ~2001)

Research ticket: 01-unearth-the-real-secret-mission-details
Date: 2026-08-14
Scope: identify the stickman platformer (ladders, ropes, gaps, levels) remembered as "Secret Mission" on an "Alcatel One Touch 311L" bought in Romania ~2001.

## Summary

- **The game is almost certainly "Run Run"** (also written "Run Run Run") — a Lode Runner–style platformer **burned into the firmware** of the Alcatel One Touch 311/310 (and the 511/512/525 line). The player's description — a stickman figure climbing ladders, hanging/swinging on ropes, jumping gaps between platforms, with levels — matches Lode Runner gameplay exactly (the player character in Lode Runner is a stickman; level tiles include ladders and horizontal rope/bars; enemies chase you; you dig trap holes; a door opens when all items are collected). Confidence: **high**.
- **"Secret Mission" is NOT a documented title** for any Alcatel phone game. No source ties that title to this phone; it is best treated as a misremembered title. Confidence: **high** (negative claim).
- The **Alcatel One Touch 311** (announced 2001, monochrome 96x65) shipped **3 games "on some versions"** (GSMArena): a space shooter (Killer Exp, "Space Impact-like"), tic-tac-toe (Eyes & Star), and Run Run. Games were part of the firmware and were **not documented in the user manual** — the official 80-page OT-310/311 manual has no games chapter. Confidence: **high**.
- **The "311L" does not exist.** The closest documented phones are the One Touch 310 (Russia-market, no WAP) and One Touch 311 (international, WAP 1.1); they share one manual ("ONE TOUCH 310 - 311") and near-identical firmware. The game lineup was operator/version dependent ("on some versions"), which is why some owners report "no games" and others remember them. Confidence: **high**.
- **The number of levels in Run Run is not documented anywhere.** Sources confirm it has levels/stages ("primer nivel", "lots of stages", save slots, score caps at 999) but no level count appears in any source found. Confidence on "not documented": **high**; the count itself: **unknown**.

## Findings by claim

### 1. The phone: Alcatel One Touch 311 (no "311L")
- GSMArena (archived 2004): "Alcatel One Touch 311 — Announced 2001 … Monochrome graphic 96 x 65 pixels … Games: 3 - on some versions". Source: `http://web.archive.org/web/20040608232515/http://www.gsmarena.com:80/alcatel_one_touch_311-285.php` — Confidence: **high** (primary spec DB, archived page read directly).
- The official combined manual is titled "ONE TOUCH 310 - 311" (Alcatel ref. 3DS 08349AAAA 01, © ALCATEL BUSINESS SYSTEMS 2001); the two models share hardware/firmware. Sources: full French guide `http://web.archive.org/web/20041204193216/http://www.my-onetouch.com:80/common/guides/ot311/alcatel_ot311_french_guide.pdf`; English quick guide `http://web.archive.org/web/20031102031333/http://www.my-onetouch.com:80/common/guides/ot311/alcatel_ot311_englisha_guide.pdf` — Confidence: **high** (official Alcatel documents).
- Russia-market relationship: mobile-review's OT-311 model page says the 311 "во многом напоминает Alcatel 511" and the 310 was the Russia variant without WAP browser. Source: `https://mobile-review.com/phonemodels/alcatel/alcatel-311.shtml` — Confidence: **medium** (secondary, but specialist).
- Firmware relationship 310/311 (games are part of the flashed software; flashing extra languages can cost you games): onliner.by forum, July 2002: "Alcatel 311 отличается от Alcatel 310 только тем, что там прошиты дополнительные (не базовые) языки, причем ради прошивки нового языка приходится жертвовать игрушками". Source: `https://forum.onliner.by/viewtopic.php?t=25856` — Confidence: **medium** (forum post from the era, plausible and consistent).
- Mobile Phone Museum listing: `https://www.mobilephonemuseum.com/phone-detail/one-touch-311` — basic specs, no games. Confidence: **high** (that games are absent there = no contradiction).
- **No "311L"** found in any consulted archive (GSMArena, PhoneDB, phonemuseum, official guides index). Romania was an official market for this phone (Romanian user guide existed; Romania listed among Alcatel country sites). Source: guides index `http://web.archive.org/web/20030627174957/http://www.my-onetouch.com:80/worldwide/com/common/userguides/ot311/1318.shtml` (26 languages incl. Romanian — the Romanian PDF itself is not archived) — Confidence: **high** for 310/311 being the real family, **medium** that the Romanian-market unit carried the games.

### 2. The games on the OT-311: three built-in, firmware-burned
- GSMArena: "Games: 3 - on some versions" (same source as above) — **high**.
- The OT-310/311 user manual (full French guide, 80 pp.) contains **no games section at all** — TOC covers calls, directory, vibrator, messages, "Mes choix", alarms, calculator/converter, settings, WAP only. Source: full French guide (above) — **high**. This explains why games are undocumented: they were a firmware/operator feature, not in the manual.
- Hands-on video 1 (Movistar/Spain unit, games demo at 08:04–10:30): main menu lists "games"; reviewer demos a space shooter "like Space Impact from Nokia" (keys 5 to shoot, 2/8 to move) plus two more games, one with "configuration difficulty sounds keyboard", one rendered in a "really small screen". Source: `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TYVYNRqNGDE` ("Alcatel OT 311 retro review - ringtones, games, wallpapers...", Yadratorex, 17 Aug 2018; auto-caption transcript read) — **high** for 3 games existing on a 311; **medium** for game identities (captions only).
- Hands-on video 2 (OT-311 bought on eBay; his first phone): "some fun games on it and it is sort of like a space impact copy … this was my favorite is kind of like a copy of loadrunner … lots of stages … collect all these little stars … while being killed by those little monsters … door opens up on the side of the screen … you have to exit the area … trap holes … you can save the game after you have a few slots". Source: `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y53pny0PfZo` ("Alcatel One Touch OT-311 from 2001 review", Vintage phones archive, 7 Mar 2017; auto-caption transcript read) — **high** for the Lode Runner–clone game on the OT-311.

### 3. Game names: Killer Exp, Eyes & Star, Run Run
- Mobile-Review's Alcatel 511 review (same platform family as 311 per mobile-review): "В телефоне также есть калькулятор, конвертор валют и три игры … killer Exp это классическая аркада, в которой нужно лететь на космическом корабле по туннелю и убивать все живое на пути. В игре eyes&star … это римейк крестиков-ноликов. Последняя игра Run Run, здесь нужно бегать по куче лабиринтов и собирать всякую всячину." (calculator, currency converter and three games: Killer Exp = tunnel space shooter; Eyes & Star = tic-tac-toe remake; Run Run = run through mazes collecting stuff). Source: `https://mobile-review.com/review/alcatel-511.shtml` (Eldar Murtazin, ~2002) — **high** for the 511 line; **medium-high** that the 311 carries the same three (mobile-review explicitly frames the 311 as a simplified 511).
- The Alcatel 525 review repeats the identical game roster (Killer Exp / Eyes&Star / Run Run). Source: `https://mobile-review.com/review/alcatel-525.shtml` (10 Jan 2003) — **high** that these are the standard Alcatel monochrome-line games.
- Independent confirmation of the name on another Alcatel of the same era: video "Juego 'Run-Run' del Alcatel 331a" — "cómo se llama ese juego que no me acuerdo… run run… run run run, nombre tan simple"; gameplay: "primer nivel superado", creatures "que te seguían", "Dónde está la puerta", score reached 999, "me queda una vida"; the same phone also had "uno de navecita y uno de tatetí" (a spaceship game and tic-tac-toe) — i.e. the same three-game roster. Source: `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i53vau9x8EE` (26 Jun 2017, 40K views) — **high** for the name and roster.
- VK post titled "Run Run Run (200?), Alcatel" with #oldschool #mobile. Source: `https://vk.com/wall-33916941_24888` (content not fetchable anonymously; title/hashtags from Yandex SERP) — **medium**.
- Helpix user reviews of the OT-311: one reviewer says "Нет игр" (no games — consistent with "some versions"), another praises the game ("Игра в одно касанье - это высший класс"). Sources: `https://helpix.ru/alcatel/one_touch_311/opinion/0004.html` and `.../0006.html` — **medium** (confirms version-dependent firmware games).

### 4. Gameplay match to the player's memory
- Lode Runner (Broderbund, 1983) gameplay: a stickman figure climbs ladders, hangs/traverses horizontal ropes (monkey bars), jumps gaps, digs trap holes to trap enemies, collects items, exits through a door; 150 levels in the original; the runner is a stickman with a headband. Run Run on the 311 is described by owners precisely this way (video 2 quote above). Confidence: **high** for the identification by gameplay; the "swinging on ropes" element maps to Lode Runner's rope/bar tiles.
- "Secret Mission": no connection found to any Alcatel firmware game in any consulted source (Yandex/Bing searches for `alcatel "secret mission"`, J2ME archives were out of scope as the game is firmware-burned, not Java). If the player ever saw the title on screen, it is not recorded anywhere findable; a misremembered or localized menu label is the most plausible explanation. Confidence: **high** that no such title is documented.

### 5. Developer / publisher
- Firmware and its games were produced by **Alcatel Business Systems** (the OT-310/311 manual is © ALCATEL BUSINESS SYSTEMS 2001, ref. 3DS 08349AAAA 01). No third-party game developer is credited for the 311's games in any source found; "Run Run" is a Lode Runner concept clone, not a licensed product (no license/attribution documented). Confidence: **medium** (no explicit credit found; "in-house firmware" is the only documented attribution).

## Level count

- **Not documented.** No source gives the number of levels/stages in Run Run on the OT-311 (or the 511/331a siblings). Owners' evidence only shows: "lots of stages" (OT-311 video), "primer nivel superado" (first level beaten, 331a video), score caps at 999, a save system with "a few slots". The classic Lode Runner has 150 levels, but the Alcatel port's level count cannot be verified from any published source. If the player wants the count, the only way to get it is from a working phone/firmware dump or by asking the video owners (Yadratorex, Vintage phones archive).

## What remains unknown

- **Number of levels in Run Run** — undocumented in every source found (see Level count).
- Whether the **Romanian-market (Connex/Orange/Diálog-era) OT-311 firmware included the games** — GSMArena says "3 games on some versions"; helpix owners show both with and without games; no Romania-specific documentation surfaced. Given the player played it, their unit evidently had them.
- Exact game roster on every operator version (e.g., whether Movistar's third game differs from other markets) — only partial hands-on evidence exists.
- The original developer of the games (in-house Alcatel team vs. licensed contractor) — not credited anywhere.
- Screenshots: none found in static form; gameplay is visible in the three YouTube videos above (OT-311 games demo at 08:04–10:30 in video 1; Lode Runner clone demo in video 2).
